Where the Protocol v1.3 and the earlier Data Collection Plan (14 Sept 2026) disagree, the protocol was followed.

## Animated visuals (game presentation)

The game layer is deliberately rich (a boot splash, an ambient particle field, HUD status chips, an animated scene above every incident, a decode effect on the analyst's lines, lock-in and stamp effects). The rules that keep it inert as an experimental factor:

- **No information.** No scene, icon or animation shows a number, a severity, a trend, a gauge, a traffic-light colour, or anything that could be read as the "right" answer. The palette is amber and blue only. The five incident scenes are themed by domain (network map, route map, ledger, scales and seals, globe) and are the same in every condition because they only know the domain, never the condition or any decision.
- **No feedback.** The lock-in effect on "Log decision" is identical whichever option was chosen; nothing on screen changes in response to the content of a decision.
- **Same timing for everyone.** The decode effect on Dr. Vale's briefing finishes at a fixed 900 ms regardless of the text length, so the epistemic and aleatory briefings are revealed identically.
- **Timed screens are not animated in.** The incident card has no entrance fade and no scan sweep: the situation report is fully legible at first paint. The animated scene sits behind the card's header as a backdrop band rather than as a block above the text, and on cards at least 640 px wide the three options sit in one row, so at 1280×720 and 1366×768 the whole situation report and all three options are on screen at render without scrolling (measured in all four conditions, see the review notes in `docs/REVIEW_NOTES.md`).
- **Latency clock.** `lat_n` starts at the first frame painted after the incident renders (double `requestAnimationFrame`), and the click times come from the input event's own timestamp (`event.timeStamp`), so a busy main thread cannot delay either end of the measurement.
- **Reduced motion.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the splash is skipped, the ambient canvas draws one static frame, and every effect sets its final state immediately.
- **No third-party resources.** All art is inline SVG, CSS and Canvas in `public/fx/`; the page still makes no external request.
- **Failure-safe.** Every effect is wrapped so that if it throws, the study flow continues unchanged. While a lock-in or badge effect plays, the inputs it has already captured are disabled and the handler is one-shot, so a second click cannot log a second decision or skip an item.
- **Internal Review scene.** The audit scene shows abstract ledger rows, a dial and a document with a seal and small check marks settling into place. These are thematic dressing for an item that itself states "No material findings were identified"; the item's response is a pre-registered attention check, not a scored decision, so the scene carries no information about the correct response beyond the item's own instruction.

## Other choices worth knowing

- **Decision options** are rendered in the fixed order Increase / Maintain / Decrease with identical styling; each carries a small monochrome glyph (up arrow, two bars, down arrow) that only restates its label, and the selected option is highlighted in the same amber whichever it is. Nothing on screen reacts to the content of a decision: there is no score, meter, feedback or consequence narrative.
- **Game presentation.** The participant works at a "CRO operations desk": a HUD with a day clock and a timeline (Sign-in, Onboarding, Briefing, Incidents 01–05, Debrief, Sign-off), an executive badge that fills in during onboarding, Dr. Vale on a "secure line" portrait panel, and each scenario as an incident card with a situation report. All of it is identical across conditions and the clock times are fixed labels, not measured time. The data-quality item is "Incident 03" in exactly the same template as the four scored scenarios.
- **Changing a decision** is allowed until Continue is pressed; the confidence rating appears only after a first choice.
- **Confidence scale** labels only the end points (1 = not at all confident, 5 = very confident), as in Appendix B.5.
- **Scenario text with a corrected typo.** Appendix B, Scenario 3, condition D reads "taken due non-compliance"; the build reads "taken due to non-compliance" so that B and D differ only in the intended ways. Every other sentence is as in the protocol, including small protocol-level wording differences between C and D in Scenarios 3 and 4.
- **Scenario text versions.** The protocol v1.3 wording ("material regulatory fines", "material contract penalties", "above average rates") is used, not the earlier working-paper wording with dollar figures and "25%" that appears in the Development Record Part III §9.
- **Manipulation-check framing.** Protocol §6 says the two items are "embedded in the game's closing narrative". The only narrative added is a heading ("End of day one") and one sentence saying Dr. Vale asks how the day's briefings came across. The items and options are verbatim.
- **Consent log linkability.** The consent record and the session row are written seconds apart. The protocol accepts a timestamp in the consent log; if the team prefers to weaken time-based matching, `CONSENT_TIMESTAMP_PRECISION=hour` truncates the consent timestamp.
- **Session timeout.** None. A participant who leaves a tab open for hours and returns will have a long `duration_sec` and a long latency on that scenario; the `hidden_n` flag and the pre-specified latency ceiling are the tools for handling this at analysis.
- **Double submission.** Submit and withdraw are each accepted once; a second attempt returns 409 and the client treats it as already recorded.
